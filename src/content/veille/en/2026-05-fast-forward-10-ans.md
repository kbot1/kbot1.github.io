## Background

The 5-year FAST-Forward results had already led many countries to adopt 26 Gy in 5 fractions. But concern about late effects, which emerge late with large fraction sizes, still held some teams back.

## Design

4,087 patients (UK trial) randomised to 40 Gy in 15 fractions, 27 Gy in 5 fractions or 26 Gy in 5 fractions to the breast or chest wall. The paper also reports a substudy of 466 patients needing axillary irradiation.

## Results

- Median follow-up: 10.1 years.
- 10-year ipsilateral breast recurrence: 3.6% (40 Gy), 2.9% (27 Gy) and 2.1% (26 Gy).
- Clinician-assessed moderate or marked late breast or chest wall effects: 13.1%, 19.3% and 14.4%. The 27 Gy arm remains the least well tolerated.
- Axillary substudy (median follow-up 7 years): 32 locoregional recurrences. The authors call these figures reassuring but still imprecise.

## What it changes

The 10-year follow-up answers the main objection to ultra-hypofractionation: 26 Gy in 5 fractions remains safe and effective. It is established as a standard for breast or chest wall irradiation. For nodal irradiation the data remain more limited, and the 15-fraction schedule, reinforced by HypoG-01, is still the most robust reference.
