## Background

Axillary dissection is increasingly omitted when the sentinel node holds only micrometastases after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (ypN1mi). There are no randomised data for this situation, and the Alliance A011202 trial has not yet reported.

## Design

Retrospective cohort of 1,585 ypN1mi patients from 84 centres in 30 countries: 804 had axillary dissection and 781 did not. Nearly 80% received nodal irradiation. Primary endpoint: axillary recurrence.

## Results

- Median follow-up: 3.1 years.
- 3-year axillary recurrence was very low (around 1 to 2%), with no difference by extent of axillary surgery.
- Triple-negative tumours: 8.7% axillary recurrence without dissection vs 2.4% with dissection (p=0.018).
- On multivariable analysis, omitting nodal irradiation was associated with a more than doubled risk of axillary recurrence (HR 2.62; 95% CI 1.19 to 5.73). Omitting dissection was not (HR 0.86).

## What it changes

Two messages for radiation oncologists. Nodal irradiation remains key when dissection is omitted in ypN1mi patients. In triple-negative disease, caution is needed: discuss dissection at the tumour board. These are retrospective data and randomised trials are awaited, but they already inform decisions.
