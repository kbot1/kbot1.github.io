## Background

In late 2025, SUPREMO (*New England Journal of Medicine*) showed no 10-year overall survival benefit from postmastectomy chest wall irradiation in intermediate-risk patients (pN1, or pN0 with risk factors): 81.4% vs 81.9%. The 2026 paper completes the picture with quality of life.

## Design

Quality-of-life substudy in the trial's UK patients: 989 agreed to take part and 620 completed the 5-year questionnaire. Original randomisation: chest wall irradiation or none.

## Results

- More chest wall symptoms after irradiation (p=0.017), improving gradually between years 1 and 5.
- No difference in arm symptoms, body image, fatigue, pain, global quality of life, physical functioning or anxiety-depression.
- In a subgroup, chest wall symptoms were worse in patients who had axillary clearance after a positive sentinel node and then irradiation.

## What it changes

The quality-of-life cost of chest wall irradiation is limited and local, but real. In the intermediate-risk population, where a survival benefit is not shown, these data support shared decision-making. Two caveats raised by ASTRO: all pN+ patients had axillary dissection, and only the chest wall was irradiated, whereas current practice often includes the regional nodes.
