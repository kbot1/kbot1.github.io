## Background

A few weeks after the MD Anderson trial, the UK TORPEdO trial gave a second randomised answer on protons in oropharyngeal cancer, with endpoints focused on function and quality of life.

## Design

205 patients with locally advanced oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma at 20 NHS hospitals, randomised 2:1 to intensity-modulated proton therapy (136) or IMRT (69). Both arms received 70 Gy in 33 fractions, 56 Gy to elective volumes and two cycles of cisplatin 100 mg/m². Two primary endpoints at 12 months:

- gastrostomy dependence or severe weight loss;
- the UW-QoL physical score.

## Results

- Gastrostomy dependence: 2% in both arms. Severe weight loss: 18% (protons) vs 6% (IMRT). No significant difference on the combined endpoint (p=0.079).
- UW-QoL physical score: 78.3 vs 77.1 (p=0.56).
- At 24 months, locoregional control 94% vs 97% and overall survival 95% in both arms.

## What it changes

With modern, well-optimised IMRT, late toxicity is low and protons bring no measurable benefit at one year. The two 2026 trials reach nuanced and partly diverging conclusions. IMRT remains the standard wherever protons are not routinely available, and proton indications should be discussed case by case pending long-term follow-up.
