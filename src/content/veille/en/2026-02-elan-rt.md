## Background

Split-course hypofractionated schedules are widely used in frail older patients, with no randomised validation until now.

## Design

202 patients aged 70 or over, assessed as frail on geriatric evaluation, with stage II-IV head and neck squamous cell carcinoma treated with curative-intent radiotherapy alone, at 30 centres in France and Monaco. Median age: 82. Randomisation between:

- 70 Gy in 35 fractions over 7 weeks;
- 55 Gy in 20 fractions, as two 2-week courses separated by a 2-week break.

Primary endpoint: alive with a complete locoregional response at 6 months.

## Results

- Primary endpoint: 35% (split-course) vs 33% (standard). Non-inferiority was accepted.
- Acute grade 3-5 toxicity: 36% vs 47%, not significant.
- Median overall survival: 13.0 vs 18.9 months (HR 1.32; 95% CI 0.97 to 1.81).

## What it changes

This is the first randomised trial in a population usually excluded from studies. Split-course becomes a validated option for patients who cannot receive the standard schedule. The unfavourable survival trend, however, leads the authors to reserve it for patients judged unfit for conventional fractionation after geriatric assessment, rather than making it the default for all older patients.
