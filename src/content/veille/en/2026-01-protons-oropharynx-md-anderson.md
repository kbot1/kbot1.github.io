## Background

Proton therapy lowers dose to swallowing structures and salivary glands. A randomised phase 3 trial was needed to show whether this dosimetric advantage translates into clinical benefit.

## Design

440 patients with stage III-IV oropharyngeal cancer were randomised at 21 US centres to intensity-modulated proton therapy (IMPT) or IMRT. Both arms received 70 Gy in 33 fractions; concurrent systemic therapy was left to the tumour board. Primary endpoint: 3-year progression-free survival (PFS), with a 9-point non-inferiority margin.

## Results

- Median follow-up: 3.2 years.
- 3-year PFS: 82.5% (IMPT) vs 83.0% (IMRT), HR 0.88. Non-inferiority was met.
- 5-year overall survival: 90.9% vs 81.0% (HR 0.58; p=0.045), with no difference in local, regional or distant recurrence.
- Per-protocol analysis: less gastrostomy dependence (26.8% vs 40.2%), dysphagia, xerostomia and severe lymphopenia.

## What it changes

This is the first randomised trial to show a clinical benefit of protons in the oropharynx. The survival gain is an unexpected secondary endpoint, not explained by tumour control. It has been widely debated, especially as a sizeable share of patients did not receive their allocated treatment. The UK TORPEdO trial, published in March, found no late functional benefit. Protons become a credible option for oropharyngeal tumours, but not yet a universal standard.
