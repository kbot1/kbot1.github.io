## Background

Since PORTEC-2, vaginal brachytherapy has been the adjuvant standard for high-intermediate risk endometrial cancer. Molecular classification (POLE, MMR, p53, L1CAM) suggests that some patients gain nothing from it while others are undertreated.

## Design

569 patients were randomised 2:1 to molecular profile-based treatment or standard vaginal brachytherapy. In the molecular arm:

- favourable profile: observation;
- intermediate profile: brachytherapy (21 Gy in 3 fractions of 7 Gy);
- unfavourable profile: pelvic radiotherapy (45 to 48.6 Gy).

Primary endpoint: 5-year vaginal recurrence.

## Results

- Median follow-up: 58 months.
- 5-year vaginal recurrence: 4.5% (molecular arm) vs 1.6% (standard). Non-inferiority was shown: the upper bound, 5.3%, stayed below the 7% margin.
- Favourable profile: 4.1% vs 0.9%, not significant but worth watching.
- Grade ≥3 genitourinary toxicity: 1% vs 2%. No treatment-related deaths.

## What it changes

This is the first randomised trial to build the molecular profile into the adjuvant radiotherapy decision. It supports omitting brachytherapy for favourable profiles (notably POLE-mutated) and escalating to pelvic radiotherapy for unfavourable ones. The slightly higher vaginal recurrence rate under observation calls for clear patient information and close follow-up. A separate toxicity, quality-of-life and sexuality analysis appeared in the *British Journal of Cancer* in 2026.
