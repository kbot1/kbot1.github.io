## Background

For twenty years, cisplatin chemoradiotherapy has been the postoperative standard for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma at high risk of relapse. Yet nearly half of patients relapse.

## Design

French-led international GORTEC trial. 680 patients under 75, operated on for cancer of the oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx or hypopharynx, with at least one high-risk feature (extranodal extension, positive margins, ≥4 involved nodes, multiple perineural invasion). Randomisation between:

- cisplatin 100 mg/m² every 3 weeks with radiotherapy;
- the same treatment plus nivolumab: one dose before, three during chemoradiotherapy, then six maintenance cycles.

Primary endpoint: disease-free survival.

## Results

- Median follow-up: 30.3 months.
- Improved disease-free survival: HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.60 to 0.98; p=0.034), an absolute gain of about 10 points at 3 years according to the data presented at ASCO 2025.
- Treatment-related grade 4 adverse events: 10% vs 5%.
- Overall survival is not yet mature.

## What it changes

This is the first positive trial of immunotherapy combined with radiotherapy in locally advanced head and neck cancer. It could make nivolumab a new postoperative standard for high-risk, cisplatin-eligible patients. The effect on overall survival remains to be seen, as does how this approach fits with the perioperative pembrolizumab strategy of KEYNOTE-689.
